Board & Advisory

The counterpart every difficult decision deserves.

Owners and CEOs rarely lack information. What’s scarce is an experienced counterpart — someone to test the difficult decisions against before they are made.

01Where we contribute

One counterpart, three places at the table

Board seat

Many owner-led companies run for years with a paper board — the legal minimum, no outside voice. An external member changes the meeting: questions get asked that insiders have stopped asking, and decisions are documented, followed up and owned.

Advisory board

A small group of outside voices around the owner or chair, without a formal board mandate: sharper material, difficult items prepared, someone to think out loud with before a position is taken.

Confidential counterpart

The more consequential the decision, the fewer people you can discuss it with. A standing sparring relationship gives you a counterpart whose only stake is your judgement improving — on a fixed rhythm, and in between when something can’t wait.

02Why companies choose it

Why companies choose it

Independence you can trust

03The board year

From formal seat to confidential counterpart

Board assignments follow the company’s formal cycle: the annual board plan, scheduled meetings, committee work where relevant, and availability between meetings for matters that can’t wait.

Advisory and sparring roles run on a standing monthly rhythm, with an agenda you control. Every role is agreed in writing, time-boxed and reviewed openly each year: if the value isn’t obvious, neither of us should continue.

Everything discussed stays in the room. That is the precondition for the whole arrangement working.
